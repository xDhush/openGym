**Evidence strength.** Strong evidence: the model cards and official documentation establish what each model outputs, where it is out of scope and how fast it ran on old devices. Strong evidence from peer-reviewed validation shows sagittal hip and knee angles in lab gait within roughly 2-8 degrees of motion capture (Stenum 4.0/5.6/7.4°; Washabaugh 3.7-5.1°), and a meta-analysis of markerless 3D gait capture rates hip and knee sagittal agreement moderate to excellent, ankle poor, and frontal and transverse planes under-studied ([Scataglini et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11175331/); CR 196, S2 183). The often-repeated claim that occluded-side errors are "roughly double" could not be found in that paper and is dropped. Weak evidence: MediaPipe's knee-valgus error of 18.83-19.68° in single-leg landings ([Heliyon 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11399566/); CR 12, S2 11), the confidence-gating study (n = 15), and an OpenPose camera-angle study that found significant biases between viewing angles and back views best ([Baldinger et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39943438/); CR 23, S2 21). Vendor fairness tables are a self-evaluation on in-house photos and conflict between Google's two cards; an independent Sony AI fairness paper could not be verified and is not used. Opinion: the camera framing proposals below.

**Key takeaways for Delva.** Use MediaPipe Pose Landmarker on both platforms so there is one skeleton and one rule set, with Apache-2.0 weights and heel points. Treat sagittal measures (depth, hip line, elbow angle, heel lift) as the cue-worthy set and treat frontal-plane valgus and lumbar rounding as coarse flags at most. Gate every cue on landmark confidence. Measure speed, heat and battery on Delva's own device matrix, because no benchmark exists for 2024-2026 mid-range phones or for 20-minute sessions; Qualcomm's AI Hub Models repo (1,257 stars, BSD-3-Clause; per-model licences not stated centrally) helps benchmark Snapdragon devices but is not a licence shortcut. Validate on the front camera, at home, on floor exercises and by gender, skin tone, age band and body size, because the model was trained on back-facing cameras and no study covers planks, bridges, dead bugs or bird dogs.

**Evidence strength.** Strong evidence: the licence texts (read directly) and the false-alarm mechanism from human factors. Weak evidence: every form-error result above (tiny subject counts, staged errors, gym lifts), the Kaia validation (n = 24, conflicts of interest) and the vision-language results (one preprint, one workshop paper). Other datasets the notes checked are less relevant: MM-Fit (no form labels; data unlicensed), UI-PRMD (CR 166) and KIMORE (CR 171) (rehab movements, depth sensors, scores rather than error types), and Pose Trainer (preprint, GPL-3.0, unmaintained, built on non-commercial OpenPose). No published technique criteria were found for glute bridges, dead bugs, bird dogs, side planks, mountain climbers or jumping jacks; screening checklists such as LESS ([Padua 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546509343200); CR 553) and 2D frontal-plane knee rating ([Munro 2012](https://doi.org/10.1123/jsr.21.1.7); CR 203) cover only squats, lunges and landings. Opinion: the architecture below.

**Key takeaways for Delva.** Build pose, then a camera-view check (do not judge an error the current view cannot see), then per-exercise rules on joint angles and timing with confidence gating, then a cue policy (one cue at a time, only after an error repeats over several reps). Start with errors that are geometric and visible in one plane: partial range or depth, hip sag or pike, heel lift; keep valgus, lumbar flexion, head position and elbow flare as low-confidence. Collect and label Delva's own consented clips with two or more qualified raters per rep, report inter-rater agreement as the ceiling, and report per-error precision, recall and kappa by view, body type, light and clothing. Keep form feedback advisory: it must never decide chapter credit, because a false positive would then cost real progress.

**Evidence strength.** Strong evidence: venues and benchmark numbers of the CVPR/ACCV papers (but cross-paper comparisons are unreliable, as the RepNet authors' note shows) and the licence texts. Weak to moderate evidence: the Oliosi phone study (lab, students, short trials). Weak evidence: a self-reported 98.89% from a phone counting preprint ([Pūioio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02420); S2 4) and PushupBench, where video-language models count push-ups poorly ([arXiv 2604.23407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23407); S2 1). Opinion: every rule for partial reps, alternating sides, tempo, pauses and hold grace periods, because no validated standard exists.

**Key takeaways for Delva.** Ship pose plus per-exercise key-pose classifiers trained on Delva's own clips, plus a hysteresis state machine; keep class-agnostic video counters for offline labelling. Define full, partial and no rep with a range-of-motion check, count only full reps toward required work, and save partial reps as activity, which matches the spec. Run one state machine per side for alternating moves and make screen and voice use the same "per side" or "per pair" convention; prefer time targets for fast moves such as mountain climbers. For holds, start after about a second in pose, pause rather than stop on brief breaks, and do not subtract a short grace period (design proposals to test). Report normalised MAE and OBO for comparability, plus a "credit agreement" metric: the share of sets where the counter and a human agree on done versus partial under Delva's rule. Because an error of about one rep on five-rep sets is common, credit a set as done at a threshold below 100% or on user confirmation, and always offer a one-tap correction so a miscount never becomes lost progress.
