**Evidence strength.** Strong evidence that the classic rules (reduced feedback frequency, self-controlled feedback, external focus, and the motivational claims of OPTIMAL theory) are unproven: bias-corrected meta-analyses put them near zero, and a Bayesian re-analysis estimated the average power of the underlying studies at 6% ([McKay et al. IRSEP](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.31234/osf.io/3nhtc); CR 39, S2 38). Strong evidence that per-rep feedback improves acute performance in trained lifters (Weakley). Weak evidence for everything specific to Delva: the guidance hypothesis itself ([Salmoni et al. 1984](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.95.3.355); CR 1,099) and feedback-after-good-trials ([Chiviacowsky and Wulf 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17479573/); CR 199) are foundational but small; working-memory overload from many instructions comes from children ([Buszard 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28878701/); CR 58); verbal encouragement every 20 s helped in one maximal test while about a third of participants viewed encouragement neutrally or negatively ([Midgley 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28737294/); CR 67); a scoping review of 49 studies found music the most studied strategy and verbal encouragement only 12% ([Ketelhut 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40858277/)); one small RCT found internal focus grew elbow flexors more (+12.4% vs +6.9%, n = 30) ([Schoenfeld 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29533715/)). Reviews support auditory information as feasible and effective in sport and rehab ([Schaffert 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30809175/); CR 182) and found no learning benefit from added vibration ([van Breda 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28761708/); CR 55). Opinion or industry practice: WCAG 1.4.2 Audio Control requires a way to pause or set the volume of audio that plays for more than 3 seconds ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/audio-control.html)), applied to native apps by analogy. No study tested counting aloud, cues per set, or how Deaf and hard-of-hearing users want cues delivered.

**Key takeaways for Delva.** Speak counts and hold milestones every rep (outcome feedback is cheap, low-risk and helps effort). Gate corrective cues by bandwidth, confidence and persistence (two or more reps), with one corrective theme per set; that budget is opinion, not evidence, and should be tested. Use graded, specific, external wording and a short positive confirmation once a fix lands, without claiming learning gains. Keep audio latency for counts well under the ~180 ms average detection threshold, with pre-rendered clips. Put richer visual summaries between sets, where visual feedback's advantage can be used. Offer every cue through at least two channels (voice, watch haptics, large on-screen text), separate volume controls for coach voice, counts and music, and an easy mute, and measure mute and ignore rates as the field signal of cue fatigue.

**Key takeaways for Delva.** The 29 Oct spec already defines content cells as program x level x days, so age and gender have no defined content effect; collecting them "for grouping" adds onboarding length, privacy surface and fairness risk with no benefit. Use age only as the 18-64 eligibility gate (and pregnancy, surgery and symptoms as safety filters, Area I). Make gender optional, inclusive and non-prescriptive. Assign by goal, level and days with a rule-based mapping, then let the first one or two sessions act as a camera-measured calibration that can suggest a level change the user accepts or declines. Explain each suggestion in one line tied to the user's own answers and allow switching any time, which also keeps decisions from being "solely automated". Audit adherence, completion and switch rates by gender, age band and body size, and watch whether women or 50-64-year-olds pick Build Strength less often under neutral copy and imagery.

**Evidence strength.** Strong evidence: ACSM 2026, WHO 2020, PAG 2018, the Currier network meta-analysis, Pelland, the frequency meta-analyses, Momma (observational association), Bosquet, Konrad, and HIIT efficacy (VO2max +5.5 ml/kg/min vs no exercise and +1.2 vs continuous training in 18-45-year-olds; [Milanović 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26243014/); CR 777). Weak evidence: the bodyweight RCTs (Kotarsky, Kikuchi, and a 5-week elastic-band push-up trial in experienced students, [Calatayud 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24983847/)), the deload RCT (a week off mid-programme slightly reduced lower-body strength gains but not size; [Coleman 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/)), 3 vs 6 days a week in trained men ([Šarić 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30363041/)), early hypertrophy after about 20 days ([Seynnes 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17053104/)) and stair-climbing "exercise snacks" ([Jenkins 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30649897/)); a 2025 exercise-snacks meta-analysis and a 2025 older-adult bodyweight meta-analysis could not be traced to primary sources and are not used. Warm-ups improved performance in 79% of examined criteria ([Fradkin 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19996770/); CR 293), while active cool-downs did little for recovery or injury ([Van Hooren and Peake 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29663142/); CR 108). Opinion or industry practice: deload consensus from strength coaches ([Bell 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37730925/)), the HIIT adoption debate ([Biddle and Batterham 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26187579/)), and bodyweight progression ladders (leverage, range, unilateral, tempo), which are coaching practice; the ACSM 2009 rule "add 2-10% when 1-2 reps over target" ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/)) maps to "move up a variation" only by analogy. ACSM 2009's "60-70% 1RM, 1-3 sets, 1-3 min rest" and Ebben 2011's push-up load percentages are unverified.

**Key takeaways for Delva.** Author programs in weekly sets per muscle (indirect work at half credit), progress by reps up to a ceiling of about 12 and then a harder variation, and cue effort "a couple of reps short of failure" rather than demanding failure. Use the first easier sets of the main movement as the warm-up and keep the cool-down short and honest (relaxation and range work, not "recovery"). For Go Further, prefer self-paced "hard but controlled" intervals with a steady alternative, and gate vigorous intervals per Area I. Frame the 8-week program as a first block with the next one ready, since gains fade when training stops. Be candid that bodyweight training lacks stand-alone trials; that is a reason to collect Delva's own outcome data, not to over-claim.
