# Measure the user, not the demographic

Delva's strongest path is an on-device MediaPipe Pose Landmarker (BlazePose GHUM 3D, Apache-2.0 code and weights) feeding a small set of per-exercise, confidence-gated rules, validated on Delva's own consented recordings. That path is forced by two findings: every public dataset with form-error labels forbids or does not permit commercial use, and the only peer-reviewed test of phone-camera rep counting found an error of about one rep on five-rep sets, with results swinging on where the phone stood ([Oliosi et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12978916/)). The training evidence supports most of the 29 Oct 2026 spec: three to five short sessions sit above the two-days-a-week strength floor and inside the trial base of the new ACSM 2026 Position Stand ([Currier et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)), the "sequence, not calendar" plan matches the best field experiments on flexible scheduling and on welcoming people back after a miss ([Milkman et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/); [Beshears et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8734590/)), and the chapter check-in is a recognised behaviour-change technique. Two decisions are challenged: grouping users by age and gender has no support inside the 18-64 band and adds proxy-bias, inclusion and legal exposure, and 15-20 minute sessions cannot reach aerobic minute targets on their own. Textbook motor-learning rules that would ration feedback have shrunk to small or null effects after bias correction, so the real limit on audio cues is false alarms from an imperfect camera model. Privacy exposure comes less from the camera (if frames never leave the device and nobody is identified) than from check-ins, screening answers and the workout log, which EU case law treats broadly as health data. openGym is the most popular comparable open project, but it has no camera features, so it serves only as a yardstick for credibility and popularity.

**Scope, assumptions and method.** No clarifying questions were asked. Launch markets are assumed to be the EU/EEA, the UK and the US. Every star count, commit date, licence and citation count was checked on **2026-11-07**. Citation counts are Crossref cited-by counts (written **CR**) and Semantic Scholar counts (written **S2**), each labelled by source; Google Scholar was not reachable. "S2 not obtained" means the Semantic Scholar API rate-limited the request. GitHub contributor counts are the number of distinct commit-author emails in each repository's git history, an approximation (written "~N contributors"), because GitHub's contributor pages were not reachable. Several primary sources (some EU and US legal texts, the EDPB and ICO sites, some publisher pages) could not be reached; claims that depend on them are marked **unverified** rather than asserted. A first-pass research round was checked by a later verification round against primary sources; where they disagreed, the verified values are used here and claims that could not be found were dropped. openGym (AGPL-3.0, 20,026 stars at the user's check on 2026-11-07, last push 2026-11-06) is not re-analysed. Evidence labels used throughout: **strong evidence** (systematic review or meta-analysis of trials, large RCT or megastudy, official guideline, binding legal text, or official product documentation for a fact about that product), **weak evidence** (single or small study, preprint, observational or lab study far from Delva's setting, narrative review), and **opinion or industry practice** (consensus without systematic grading, store or vendor practice, commentary, or this report's own inference). This is research, not legal or medical advice.

## 1. Executive summary

The table gives the one finding per area that should most change what Delva builds, with its evidence strength. Sections 2 to 5 carry the detail and the sources.

| Area | Key finding for Delva | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|
| A. Pose estimation on phones | MediaPipe Pose Landmarker is the only option that is cross-platform, Apache-2.0 for code and weights, has heel and toe points and publishes a fairness card. Single-camera 2D pose tracks sagittal hip and knee angles within a few degrees in lab gait studies, but frontal-plane knee valgus is poor (r = 0.20 against 3D) and no study validates floor exercises. Confidence gating decides whether angles are usable at all. | Strong for product facts; weak to moderate for accuracy in Delva's home setting |
| B. Form assessment | No dataset with form-error labels can be used commercially (Fitness-AQA, EgoExo-Fitness and MyoMechanix forbid it; others have no licence). The only product-level validation (Kaia, n = 24) matched physiotherapist-to-physiotherapist agreement. General vision-language models judge form barely above chance. False alarms damage trust more than misses. | Strong for licences and the false-alarm mechanism; weak for transfer to fitness cues |
| C. Rep counting and holds | Two key poses plus a hysteresis state machine is the only documented real-time phone recipe. The one peer-reviewed phone test (MoveNet) found 61% detection and an error of about 1 rep on ~5-rep sets, best from diagonal views (200 cm for squats, 90-180 cm for push-ups). Nobody has validated partial-rep rules, per-side counting or hold grace periods. | Weak to moderate |
| D. Audio feedback and motor learning | Feedback after every rep improves in-session performance (g = 0.63). The "give less feedback", self-controlled, external-focus and positive-feedback rules shrink to small or null effects after bias correction. Non-musicians notice action-to-sound delays at about 180 ms on average. | Strong that the classic rules are unproven; weak for cue budgets and latency targets |
| E. Personalisation and grouping | Women and men make similar relative gains; WHO gives one recommendation for all adults 18-64; individual response varies more than group averages. Tailoring helps a little (r = .074 print, d = .139 web) and works through behaviour, not demographics. Demographic proxies cause group bias. | Strong |
| F. Program design | ACSM 2026: progressive resistance training works with variable prescription; failure, periodisation, equipment and set structure do not consistently matter; strength needs at least 2 sessions a week. Bodyweight training was never studied on its own except the Nordic curl. Detraining grows with break length. | Strong (guidelines and meta-analyses), indirect for bodyweight |
| G. Adherence | Median retention in remote digital studies was 5.5 days. Flexible incentives beat fixed time slots; rewarding a return after a missed workout was the best of 54 interventions; highlighting a broken streak lowers later engagement; median habit formation is 59-66 days with a 4-335 day range. | Strong for the principles; weak for exercise-app specifics |
| H. Exercise libraries and taxonomies | wger is the only mature, governed open exercise database (AGPL code, CC-BY-SA data). No open source models ordered progressions, contraindications or coaching cues. Popular datasets hide proprietary media or weak provenance. | Strong (primary code and licence files) |
| I. Safety and screening | ACSM 2015 screens on current activity, symptoms or known disease, and intended intensity. Exercise rarely causes serious harm but often minor problems (number needed to harm 6). The licence terms of PAR-Q+ and CSEP questionnaires for apps are unverified. FDA guidance (2026 general wellness; 2022 software functions) places fitness tracking and "exercise or posture suggestions" outside device rules. | Strong for harm rates and regulator text; consensus for screening |
| J. Privacy | Pose for form feedback is not GDPR Art. 9 biometric data unless used to identify someone; check-ins, screening and logs are likely health data under CJEU C-21/23. The AI Act makes camera-based age, sex or emotion inference risky. Apple bans personal health information in iCloud; Google Play requires a Health apps declaration. | Strong for EU texts and store rules; US state laws unverified |
| K. Comparable open projects | No open app combines camera coaching with structured programs. workout.cool (MIT) has the closest program model, LiftLog the clearest progression rules, Feeel the only home timed-workout app (stale). OpenCap, Pose2Sim and Sports2D are validation tools with licence traps. | Strong (primary code) |
