**Evidence strength.** Strong evidence: rapid early drop-off (Pratap, Meyerowitz-Katz), flexibility over fixed slots (Beshears), rewarding returns (Milkman), self-monitoring combined with goal review (0.42 vs 0.26 across 122 evaluations; [Michie 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19916637/); CR 1,883), goal setting (d = .34, [Epton 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29189034/), CR 307; multi-component goal setting d = .552, [McEwan 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26445201/), CR 249), apps and trackers (+1,850 steps/day; samples only 28% women; [Laranjo 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33355160/); CR 364), and gamification (g = 0.42 overall, 0.23 vs active controls, 0.15 at ~14-week follow-up; [Mazeas 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34982715/); CR 215). Weak evidence: Kaushal and Rhodes, Perri, fresh-start effects at temporal landmarks ([Dai 2014](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901); CR 299), habit of starting rather than of executing predicting frequency ([Phillips and Gardner 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26148187/); CR 119), short bouts giving no better 18-month participation than long bouts in women ([Jakicic 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10546695/); CR 402), beginners who chose the day's workout gaining more leg-press strength (+62 vs +16 kg, n = 16; [McNamara and Stearne 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20042923/)), and the HeartSteps micro-randomised trial, where suggestions raised steps by 14% (p = .06) with effects fading over weeks ([Klasnja 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30192907/); CR 240). The classic "about half of new exercisers drop out within 6 months" traces to early 1980s studies as reviewed by [Rhodes and Sui 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8450373/), not to a verified primary figure. Papers on streak dependency, the "hidden cost" of quantification, mid-goal dips and post-milestone let-down (Stawarz 2015, Renfree 2016, Etkin 2016, Bonezzi 2011, Amir and Ariely 2008) were confirmed only bibliographically; their findings were not re-checked and are treated as hypotheses. The "what-the-hell" effect source could not be found.

**Key takeaways for Delva.** Put micro-milestones inside chapter 1 (first workout, first week) and an early light check-in after three workouts or seven days. Pair the calendar-free sequence with an optional user-chosen "when and where" plan and a friendly return message after a miss. If streaks are shown at all, make them weekly, count approved shorter versions, and offer repair. Give the shorter-version "reserve" a small visible limit. Instrument attrition from day 0 (camera setup, permissions, first and second workout, chapter boundaries) and plot curves, because none of this evidence comes from vision-based home fitness apps.

**Evidence strength.** Strong evidence: harm rates (Niemeijer), blood-pressure benefits of isometrics (Edwards), supervised-vs-unsupervised effectiveness, the pregnancy guideline's overall conclusion, and the FDA texts. Weak evidence: injury epidemiology, which mostly lacks exposure data: weight-training sports range from 0.24-1 injuries per 1,000 h (bodybuilding) to 7.5 (Highland Games) ([Keogh and Winwood 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27328853/); CR 165), CrossFit 0.2-18.9 per 1,000 h ([Rodríguez 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33322981/)), and an estimated 3,988,903 US emergency visits from HIIT-type equipment and calisthenics in 2007-2016, most often in men (58%) and people aged 20-39 (39%) ([Rynecki 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30758171/)). In cardiac rehab, events ran at 1 per 129,456 h of moderate vs 1 per 23,182 h of high-intensity exercise, with only 3 events and no heart attacks ([Rognmo 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22879367/); CR 407), which supports gating vigorous intervals for known heart disease. Opinion or consensus: ACSM screening itself, Too Fit To Fracture's conditional osteoporosis advice ([Giangregorio 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00198-014-2881-4)), and UK clinical stop-rule wording. No study gives an injury rate per 1,000 hours for unsupervised home bodyweight training, and no study tests in-app screening.

**Key takeaways for Delva.** Screen in a few questions: symptoms first (any yes pauses training until the user confirms professional advice, stored as a self-attestation), known heart, metabolic or kidney disease (allow light-to-moderate programs, lock vigorous intervals until clearance is confirmed; Delva must decide whether to follow ACSM strictly for non-exercisers), activity status, and routing for pregnancy, recent surgery or fracture. Re-screen at every chapter check-in. Model conditions as tags that swap or cap exercises rather than exclude whole programs. Add an "I feel unwell" button that ends the session (saved as partial activity) with plain stop-and-seek-help advice. Keep every claim at "helps you follow the plan": no rehab, no condition-specific programs, no "prevents injury" or "lowers blood pressure" claims, which would move Delva toward device rules.

**Key takeaways for Delva.** Keep frames in memory on the device and never write, log or upload them; keep keypoints in a short rolling window and drop face and hand landmarks inside the inference wrapper; store only derived metrics by default. Never identify users by face or movement (use explicit profile selection). Ask age and gender by form, never infer them or emotions from the camera. Make recording, upload and model-training use separate opt-ins with a visible indicator and short retention. Collect health data (check-ins, screening) only after a dedicated, granular, withdrawable explicit consent, evaluate screening on device where possible, and never hard-block automatically. Keep health data out of iCloud and away from ad or analytics SDKs, disclose any third-party AI use, run a DPIA before launch, offer a camera-free mode, and add network tests in CI that prove camera sessions send no image or keypoint traffic.

**Key takeaways for Delva.** Borrow the workout.cool hierarchy with a Chapter entity whose completion predicate is "required work done AND at least 14 active (non-paused) days", enforce idempotency (one credit per required workout), keep partial sessions as activity without credit, and record which approved short version earned credit. Borrow LiftLog's "one rule after full success" for moving between bodyweight variants and Feeel's per-side timed entries. Avoid wger's one-logged-set gate. No open app has end-of-chapter check-ins that suggest frequency changes, or an audit trail of the user's accept or decline; Delva must design those itself (Areas E and G).
