## 3. New facts that close gaps in the notes

| # | New fact | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The AI Act's "biometric data" has no identification element.** Art. 3(34) defines it as personal data "resulting from specific technical processing relating to the physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics of a natural person, such as facial images or dactyloscopic data". The GDPR's "which allow or confirm the unique identification" is absent. Recital 14 says it "should be interpreted in light of" GDPR Art. 4(14) and that biometric data "can allow for the authentication, identification or categorisation of natural persons and for the recognition of emotions". So camera keypoints are plausibly "biometric data" under the AI Act. That matters only if Delva builds one of the defined systems: identification, categorisation or emotion recognition. This sharpens J's AI Act row. | Binding law | [AI Act Art. 3(34), Rec. 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689) |
| 2 | **Body-feature "filters" can count as ancillary.** Recital 16 lists sex and age among categorisation categories. It excludes "purely ancillary" features that "cannot, for objective technical reasons, be used without the principal service", giving virtual try-on and social-media filters that "categorise facial or body features" as examples. Inferring age or sex from the camera to *assign a program* would not obviously fit this exclusion. This supports J's rule to collect age and gender by form. | Binding law (recital) | [AI Act Rec. 16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689) |
| 3 | **The Digital Omnibus rewrote the AI literacy duty.** New Art. 4(1): providers and deployers "shall take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf". The Commission must publish practical examples. J's statement that "only the AI literacy duty (Art. 4) ... appl[ies]" should use the amended wording. The Omnibus also makes Arts. 102-110 apply from 27 July 2026 and adds new Art. 5(1)(ba)/(bb) prohibitions (intimate imagery) from 2 December 2026, which are not relevant to Delva. | Binding law | [Reg. (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32026R1744) |
