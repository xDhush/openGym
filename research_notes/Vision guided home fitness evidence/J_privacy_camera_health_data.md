## Main table: the 10 most important items

### Takeaway
Three rules drive almost all of Delva's camera risk:
- **GDPR Art. 9 (EU/UK):** pose data becomes "special category biometric data" only when it is processed *for the purpose of uniquely identifying* someone.
- **Illinois BIPA:** the risk turns on whether a keypoint model is a "scan of face or hand geometry".
- **Store rules:** Apple 2.5.14 / 5.1.x and Google Play's health-app declaration.

On the health side, check-in pain answers and screening answers are health data in every regime. Workout history probably is too in the EU after C-21/23, and in Washington under MHMDA. The cleanest design is to run pose inference only in memory on the device, never identify anyone, drop facial and hand landmarks, store only derived metrics, and ask for separate explicit consent for health data.

### Inferences
- The legal "trigger words" differ by regime. GDPR, ICO, CCPA and (likely) MHMDA ask whether data is **used to identify**. BIPA and CUBI ask whether there is a **scan or record of face or hand geometry**, and courts disagree on whether identification capability is required. So the most robust single design rule is **not to produce face or hand geometry at all**: strip those landmarks inside the inference step.
- Health data exposure comes less from the camera and more from **check-ins, screening and the workout log**. The CJEU inference test and MHMDA's "inferred data" clause push the same way.
- Store rules are stricter than the law in two places that bite Delva directly: no personal health information in iCloud (Apple 5.1.3(ii)), and a recording indicator plus explicit consent for any camera use (Apple 2.5.14).

- Exact statutory text of BIPA §§15 and 20, PA 103-0769's damages clause, RCW 19.373, Texas ch. 503, Colorado HB24-1130, CCPA §1798.140 and the nFADP could not be fetched (hosts blocked; search budget exhausted). Counsel should pull them.

## Is pose/keypoint data biometric or health data? (EU, UK, US analysis)

### Takeaway
In the EU and UK, Delva's pose keypoints are *probably* "biometric data" in the Art. 4(14) sense, because they come from specific technical processing of physical and behavioural traits. They are **not Art. 9 special category biometric data unless processed to uniquely identify someone**. But outputs revealing health status (pain, injury, impaired mobility) are **health data**, and those carry the same Art. 9 burden. In the US, the BIPA and CUBI risk depends on whether facial or hand landmarks count as "face/hand geometry". The *Zellmer* line says no unless they can identify, but that is not settled Illinois law. Washington's MHMDA likely captures workout and check-in data as consumer health data whatever happens with biometrics.

### Cited Findings
**EU (GDPR)**
- Biometric data needs three elements: specific technical processing; physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics; and that the data "allow or confirm the unique identification" of the person. Art. 9 applies only to biometric data processed "for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person" — [GDPR Art. 4(14), 9(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) (U verbatim).
- Video is not Art. 9 biometric unless "specifically technically processed in order to contribute to the identification of an individual". Detecting physical characteristics to classify, without templates for unique identification, is outside Art. 9 — [EDPB Guidelines 3/2019](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en_0.pdf) (V-excerpt).
- Indirect revelation by deduction counts as special category processing — [C-184/20 OT](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62020CJ0184) (V-excerpt). Health data is read broadly, and order data for non-prescription pharmacy medicines is health data — [C-21/23 press release](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2024-10/cp240159en.pdf) (V-excerpt).
- The 2015 WP29 annex treats lifestyle or sensor data as health data when used to draw conclusions about health status or risk — [WP29 annex](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/other-document/files/2015/20150205_letter_art29wp_ec_health_data_after_plenary_annex_en.pdf) (U).
- The household exemption shields the person filming in a purely personal setting. The EDPB applied this in 2026 to smart-glasses users — [EDPB smart-glasses statement](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-10/20261310_edpb_statement_smart-glasses_en.pdf) (V-excerpt). GDPR recital 18 says the Regulation still applies to controllers or processors "which provide the means" for household processing — [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) (U).

**EU AI Act**
- Emotion recognition excludes "physical states, such as pain or fatigue" and the "mere detection of readily apparent expressions, gestures or movements, unless they are used for identifying or inferring emotions" — [AI Act recital 18](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/recital-18) (V-excerpt).
- The Commission's AI Act Service Desk says noticing someone smiling is not emotion recognition — [Service Desk: Biometrics](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/biometrics) (V-excerpt).
- The biometric categorisation definition excludes features "ancillary to another commercial service and strictly necessary for objective technical reasons" — [AI Act Art. 3, via EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AL_202401689) (V-excerpt, numbering not confirmed).

**UK (UK GDPR / ICO)**
- Biometric data is special category "only if you use it to uniquely identify someone", but it may be other special category data, "for example ... health data" — [ICO biometric recognition](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/biometric-data-guidance-biometric-recognition/biometric-recognition/) (V-excerpt).
- "If you are using biometric data but not for the purpose of uniquely identifying someone, it is not special category biometric data" — [ICO consumer IoT guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/online-tracking/guidance-for-consumer-internet-of-things-products-and-services/what-information-do-iot-products-use/) (V-excerpt; the excerpt could come from this page or the draft IoT PDF).

**Switzerland (nFADP)**
- Sensitive data includes health data and "biometric data that uniquely identifies a natural person" — [Fedlex nFADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en) (U).

**US**
- BIPA "biometric identifier" = "retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or scan of hand or face geometry", excluding physical descriptions such as height and weight — [740 ILCS 14/10](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3004&ChapterID=57) (V-excerpt). Electronic signature counts as a written release from 2 Aug 2024 — [PA 103-0769](https://ilga.gov/legislation/publicacts/103/103-0769.htm) (V-excerpt).
- *Zellmer v. Meta* (9th Cir. 2024): face signatures that cannot identify a person are not biometric identifiers or information, and age or gender predictions are not "identification" — [Hunton summary](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ninth-circuit-holds-face-signatures-used-for-photo-tag-suggestions-are-not-biometric-information), [Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/06/facebook-defeats-bipa-face-scanning-lawsuit-zellmer-v-meta.htm) (V-secondary; primary opinion not reached). One commentator calls the opinion "confusing" and notes the Ninth Circuit is not the leading interpreter of Illinois law (same Goldman source; [Opinion]).
- Apple's Vision body-pose landmarks include face points: "the nose, the left and right eye, and the left and right ear" — [WWDC20 session 10653](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10653/) (V-full).
- Texas CUBI enforcement scale: $1.4B Meta settlement — [Texas AG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-14-billion-settlement-meta-over-its-unauthorized-capture) (V-excerpt).
- MHMDA private right of action used in *Maxwell v. Amazon* (filed 10 Feb 2025) — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20250220-first-lawsuit-filed-under-washingtons-my-health-my-data-act), [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/ph-privacy/biometrics-litigation-update-first-class-action-complaint-filed-under-washingtons-my-health-my-data-act-is-your-company-ready) (V-secondary).

**Classification of Delva's data types (researcher's analysis; [Opinion], built on the findings above)**

| Delva data | EU / UK GDPR | US (BIPA / CUBI) | US (MHMDA / CCPA) | Recommended handling |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Household exemption covers the user, not Delva as provider of the means. | Photographs are excluded from BIPA, but scans of face geometry from them have been litigated (U). | Not "collected" if never leaves device (Apple's definition); state-law "collection" untested. | Process in volatile memory only. ### Inferences
- **The purpose test is Delva's main EU/UK protection, so it must be real.** If Delva ever uses movement or face features to recognise *which* household member is exercising, to detect account sharing, or to fight fraud, the processing becomes Art. 9 biometric processing and needs explicit consent.
- **Health data is unavoidable** once pain check-ins and screening exist. The question is not whether to have an Art. 9 condition but how to collect explicit consent cleanly and how far to extend it over the workout log.
- **For BIPA, a conservative engineering control beats a legal argument.** Removing facial and hand landmarks costs little for most bodyweight exercises; head and neck alignment can use shoulder and neck points. That control makes the *Zellmer* question moot.

### Gaps
- Primary text of the *Zellmer* opinion (9th Cir. No. 22-16925, believed) and Illinois Supreme Court opinions could not be fetched.
- District-court BIPA decisions on virtual try-on or face-detection-without-identification (believed to include *Daichendt v. CVS* and several try-on cases) were not verified, so the size of the split is unknown.
- No regulator guidance specifically on **pose estimation** or **skeleton data** was located (search budget exhausted). The EDPB 3/2019 and ICO analysis is applied here by analogy.
- Academic evidence that skeleton or gait sequences can re-identify people was not searched. It is flagged as a plausible risk only.

## Concrete design rules for Delva (on-device processing, frame retention, consent, DPIA)

### Takeaway
Build to the strictest common denominator:
- on-device, in-memory inference;
- no identification;
- no face or hand landmarks;
- derived metrics only;
- opt-in for any recording, upload or training use;
- explicit separate consent for health data;
- a DPIA before launch;
- no ad or analytics SDKs near health or camera data;
- privacy promises that are tested in CI.

### Cited Findings
- Apple treats data "processed only on device" as not collected, but derived data sent off device must be disclosed — [Apple App Privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/) (V-full). This rewards an on-device architecture at the store-label level.
- Apple requires "explicit user consent" and a "clear visual and/or audible indication" for "any use of the device camera" — [Apple 2.5.14](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (V-full).
- Apple bars using data from "depth and/or facial mapping tools (such as ARKit, Camera APIs, or Photo APIs)" for "marketing, advertising or use-based data mining" — [Apple 5.1.2(vi)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (V-full).
- Apple bars storing "personal health information in iCloud" — [Apple 5.1.3(ii)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (V-full).
- Apple requires disclosure and explicit permission before sharing personal data "with third-party AI" — [Apple 5.1.2(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (V-full).
- Under EDPB guidance, identification-free classification of physical characteristics stays outside Art. 9; where Art. 9 applies, Art. 9 and Art. 6 grounds are both needed and a non-biometric alternative must be offered — [EDPB 3/2019](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en_0.pdf) (V-excerpt).
- Under ICO guidance, explicit consent is the likely condition for biometric recognition — [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/biometric-data-guidance-biometric-recognition/how-do-we-process-biometric-data-lawfully/) (V-excerpt).
- Android: the camera runtime permission is "subject to while-in-use restrictions", so a camera foreground service cannot be created while the app is in the background — [Android FGS types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types) (V-full).
- The FTC punished SDK-based sharing of health data (Premom, GoodRx) and broken privacy promises (Flo) — [FTC Premom](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/05/ovulation-tracking-app-premom-will-be-barred-sharing-health-data-advertising-under-proposed-ftc), [FTC GoodRx](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/02/ftc-enforcement-action-bar-goodrx-sharing-consumers-sensitive-health-info-advertising) (V-excerpt).
- GDPR Art. 35(3) makes a DPIA mandatory for, among other things, "processing on a large scale of special categories of data" and systematic evaluation based on automated processing. The EDPB-endorsed WP248 criteria (sensitive data, innovative technology, systematic monitoring, and so on; two or more usually mean a DPIA) point the same way — [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), [WP248 rev.01](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/items/611236) (both U).
- For comparison only: openGym's own project description is "no third-party account, no telemetry", self-hosted, and it has no camera feature — [openGym CLAUDE.md in this repo](/home/user/openGym/CLAUDE.md) [Industry practice / comparison]. It shows that a zero-telemetry posture is feasible for a fitness log, but it offers no precedent for camera processing.

**Design rules (each tied to the source that motivates it)**
1. **Frames live only in memory, on device.** Camera buffers go straight to the on-device pose model. No frame is written to disk, cache, logs, crash reports or analytics. No network call carries image data. Basis: GDPR Art. 5(1)(c) and Art. 25 (U); Apple "collect" definition (V-full); FTC promise-matching (V-excerpt).
2. **Keypoints are transient and reduced.** Keep a short rolling window, a few seconds, in memory for rep and phase detection. Zero out or delete **facial landmarks (nose, eyes, ears) and hand landmarks** inside the inference wrapper before any other module sees them. Basis: BIPA "face/hand geometry" (V-excerpt); Vision includes face points (V-full).
3. **Persist derived metrics only by default.** Store rep counts, hold durations, per-rep form flags, an aggregated form score and set or session summaries. No raw keypoint time series is stored unless a user turns on a replay feature, and then body-only joints on device.
4. **No identification, ever, without a new legal analysis.** Do not use pose, movement or face to recognise users, separate household members, detect account sharing or prevent fraud. Use explicit profile selection instead. Record "no unique identification purpose" in the record of processing and the DPIA. Basis: GDPR Art. 9(1), EDPB, ICO (V-excerpt).
5. **No inference of protected traits from the camera.** Age, gender and body composition come from user-entered forms, never from the camera. Basis: AI Act biometric categorisation and Annex III (V-excerpt/U); Apple 5.1.2(vi) (V-full).
6. **No facial emotion analysis.** Effort and fatigue features must be derived from movement (tempo slowdown, form breakdown) and described as physical state. Basis: AI Act recital 18 (V-excerpt).
7. **Recording is a separate, visible opt-in.** Any clip capture (self-review, sharing with a coach, bug report) needs its own consent screen, is off by default, shows a persistent on-screen "recording" indicator, and saves on device by default. Upload happens only on an explicit per-clip action. Basis: Apple 2.5.14 (V-full).
8. **Retention schedule (proposed, [Opinion]).**
   - Frames: none beyond the inference loop.
   - Live keypoints: in-session only.
   - Derived metrics and workout history: account lifetime, deleted on account deletion (in-app, per Apple 5.1.1(v)).
   - Uploaded clips: auto-delete after a short window (for example 30 days) unless the user pins them.
   - Debug uploads: only via an explicit "send this clip to support" flow, deleted when the ticket closes.
   - If any BIPA biometric is ever retained: a public retention and destruction schedule (BIPA §15(a), U).
9. **Model training is a separate consent.** Never bundle it into the ToS. Ask for separate opt-in consent to use clips or keypoint data to improve models, withdrawable, with deletion from future training sets. Prefer pseudonymised, body-only keypoints over video. Basis: GDPR Art. 9(2)(a) if health is inferable (U); Apple 5.1.1(ii) withdrawal (V-full); MHMDA dual consent (U).
10. **Health data consent design.** Use a dedicated explicit-consent step, separate from account creation, before the first pain check-in or screening. Tie it to stated purposes ("adapt your program"), make it granular (check-ins vs. screening), and let the user withdraw it in settings. Offer a usable path without health answers: generic programs plus a "see a professional" prompt. Basis: GDPR Art. 9(2)(a) (U); MHMDA (U); Apple 5.1.1(ii)/(iv) (V-full).
11. **Screening on device where possible.** Evaluate pre-exercise screening answers locally and keep only the outcome (for example "cleared" or "consult a doctor first"). Do not hard-block users automatically; show the reason and let them proceed with a recommendation to see a doctor. Basis: Apple 1.4.1 "remind users to check with a doctor" (V-full); GDPR Art. 22(4) for decisions based on special category data (U).
12. **No health data in iCloud.** If iOS sync is offered, use Delva's own backend, not CloudKit or iCloud documents, for check-ins and health answers. Basis: Apple 5.1.3(ii) (V-full).
13. **SDK hygiene.** No advertising SDKs. Keep analytics and crash SDKs off workout, check-in and camera screens, or self-host them. Contracts must forbid secondary use. Basis: FTC GoodRx/Premom/BetterHelp (V-excerpt); Apple 5.1.3(i) (V-full); Google Play Health Connect restrictions (V-excerpt); *Maxwell v. Amazon* SDK theory (V-secondary).
14. **Third-party AI (coach or LLM).** If any workout or check-in data goes to an external AI model, disclose it and get explicit permission first. Prefer sending derived, minimised data, never frames. Basis: Apple 5.1.2(i) (V-full).
15. **DPIA before launch (EU/UK/CH).** Cover the camera pipeline (show on-device processing and frame discard), health data, profiling for program assignment, SDKs, transfers, and the AI model. Revisit it whenever recording, uploads or training are introduced. Basis: GDPR Art. 35 / WP248 (U); ICO (V-excerpt); nFADP (U).
16. **Test the promises.** Add automated tests or network-traffic checks in CI to show that camera sessions produce no outbound image or keypoint traffic. Basis: FTC Section 5 deception theory in the Flo-type cases (V-excerpt).
17. **Foreground-only camera.** Run the camera only while the session screen is visible. Do not use a `camera` foreground service unless a picture-in-picture or multitask feature truly needs it. Basis: Android while-in-use rules (V-full).
18. **Position as fitness, not medical.** No diagnosis, treatment or rehab claims, and no accuracy claims for health measurements without validation. Add a "not a medical device" disclaimer. Basis: Apple 1.4.1 (V-full); Google Play Health Content policy (V-excerpt).

### Inferences
- Rules 1-6 shrink the legal analysis to "ordinary personal data plus explicit-consent health data", which is a well-trodden path. Rules 7-9 contain the risk from any future feature that needs video.
- On-device processing does not by itself remove GDPR controllership. Delva determines the purposes and means of the on-device pipeline, so transparency and DPIA duties likely still apply even when Delva never receives the frames ([Opinion]; see open questions).

### Gaps
- **Industry practice could not be verified.** The search budget ran out before any public statements from pose-based fitness apps about on-device processing could be located and checked. Apple's WWDC20 body-pose session does not itself say processing is on device; it says only that Vision works offline on libraries of images and that inference latency depends on the device ([WWDC20 10653](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10653/), V-full). No industry-practice claims are made here.
- No regulator document was found that sets a numeric retention period for fitness video. The 30-day clip window above is the researcher's proposal ([Opinion]).

## App Store and Google Play requirements checklist

### Takeaway
Both stores require consent-first camera use and honest disclosure. Both also have a health-specific layer: Apple 5.1.3 (including the iCloud ban), and Google's Health apps declaration plus Health Connect review. Both forbid advertising use of health data, and both penalise unsupported medical accuracy claims.

### Cited Findings
**Apple (all from [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) and [App Privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), V-full unless marked)**
- [ ] Camera: explicit consent and a clear visual or audible indicator whenever recording or making a record of activity (2.5.14).
- [ ] A purpose string (`NSCameraUsageDescription`) that "clearly and completely" describes use (5.1.1(ii)). The Info.plist key name and the fact that the app terminates without it come from researcher knowledge (U), because Apple's AVFoundation page did not render.
- [ ] Privacy policy link in App Store Connect and in the app. It must state data collected, uses, third-party protections, retention and deletion, and how to revoke consent (5.1.1(i)).
- [ ] An easy way to withdraw consent. Paid features must not depend on granting data access (5.1.1(ii)).
- [ ] Data minimisation; request only what the core function needs (5.1.1(iii)). Offer alternatives when consent is refused (5.1.1(iv)), e.g. a manual rep-logging mode without the camera.
- [ ] In-app account deletion if accounts exist (5.1.1(v)).
- [ ] Submit as a legal entity, since the app needs sensitive user information (5.1.1(ix)).
- [ ] Disclose sharing with third parties "including with third-party AI" and get explicit permission (5.1.2(i)). Use ATT only if tracking, ideally never.
- [ ] Camera, ARKit and facial-mapping data never used for marketing, advertising or data mining (5.1.2(vi)).
- [ ] Health and fitness data (incl. HealthKit, Motion and Fitness) not used for advertising or data mining. Disclose the specific health data collected (5.1.3(i)).
- [ ] No false data written to HealthKit. **No personal health information stored in iCloud** (5.1.3(ii)).
- [ ] Medical accuracy claims need disclosed methodology. Remind users to consult a doctor (1.4.1).
- [ ] App Privacy label: on-device-only data need not be disclosed. Derived data sent to Delva's servers must be, likely under Fitness, Health and possibly Sensitive Info (which includes "biometric data"). Mark data "linked to user" unless de-identified before collection.
- [ ] Privacy manifest (`PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`) for the app and third-party SDKs, with required-reason API declarations ([Apple privacy manifest docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files); U, page not readable in this session).

**Google Play / Android**
- [ ] Complete the **Health apps declaration** on the App content page. Select "Activity and fitness" (and nothing medical unless true) — [Android Developers: publish](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/publish) (V-full); [Play Health Content and Services](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en) (V-excerpt).
- [ ] Complete the **Data safety** section accurately — [Android Developers: publish](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/publish) (V-full), linking [Data safety help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469).
- [ ] Meet the User Data policy: privacy policy in Play Console and in the app, plus **prominent disclosure and consent** for data behind runtime permissions such as the camera — [Play Health Content and Services](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en) (V-excerpt); [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311) (not fetched).
- [ ] If Health Connect is used: request the minimum data types, justify each, and use a privacy-policy activity that matches the Play policy (`ACTION_SHOW_PERMISSIONS_RATIONALE`). History reads are limited to 30 days unless the history permission is granted — [Health Connect get started](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/get-started) (V-full).
- [ ] Health Connect data is never used for advertising, creditworthiness or data brokers. Since the April 2026 update, it is never used for employment or insurance eligibility or unauthorized social sharing — [Play Health permissions FAQ](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12991134?hl=en), [Policy announcement 15 Apr 2026](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16926792?hl=en) (V-excerpt).
- [ ] Medical device status declared, or a "not a medical device" disclaimer added — [Play Health Content and Services](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en) (V-excerpt; scope to be checked).
- [ ] If a foreground service is used: declare the type (`camera` needs `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_CAMERA` plus the `CAMERA` grant; `health` suits exercise tracking) and declare FGS types in Play Console. A camera FGS cannot start from the background — [Android FGS types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types) (V-full).

### Inferences
- Apple's iCloud ban and Google's declaration review are the two store items most likely to cause late rework. Decide the sync backend and the Health Connect data-type list early.
- A camera-free fallback mode (manual logging or a timer) helps with Apple 5.1.1(iv) and gives GDPR consent a genuine choice.

- The Google Play User Data policy, Data safety definitions (including any "ephemeral processing" exemption) and the Permissions policy pages were not readable (support.google.com blocked). Their exact wording is unverified.
- Apple privacy-manifest dates and SDK signature rules are unverified (U).

## Open legal questions to put to counsel

### Takeaway
The open questions cluster around five issues:
- whether Delva is a controller of purely on-device processing;
- whether stored keypoints or workout logs are "biometric" or "health" data;
- BIPA's face-geometry reach;
- MHMDA's reach into fitness metrics;
- whether Delva is an HBNR "PHR vendor".

### Cited Findings
1. **Controllership of on-device processing.** If frames and keypoints never leave the device, is Delva still the controller of that processing, so that transparency, DPIA and Art. 9 analysis apply? Background: the household exemption covers the user — [EDPB 2026 statement](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-10/20261310_edpb_statement_smart-glasses_en.pdf) (V-excerpt). Recital 18 GDPR keeps providers of the means in scope — [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) (U).
2. **Stored keypoint sequences.** Do they "allow" unique identification (Art. 4(14)), which would make them biometric data even without an identification purpose? Does storing them change the Art. 9 analysis or the security duties? See [EDPB 3/2019](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en_0.pdf) (V-excerpt).
3. **Workout log as health data.** After [C-21/23](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2024-10/cp240159en.pdf), are rep counts, form flags, range-of-motion limits and skipped exercises "data concerning health"? Which Art. 6 and Art. 9 combination should apply: contract plus explicit consent, or explicit consent for everything?
4. **Screening and Art. 22.** Is gating or adapting programs on screening answers an automated decision with "similarly significant" effects based on special category data (Art. 22(4))? See [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) (U).
5. **BIPA face and hand geometry.** Do body-pose models that output 5 facial points, or hand-pose models, perform a "scan of face/hand geometry"? Does *Zellmer*'s identification requirement hold in Illinois state courts? Should Illinois users get an electronic BIPA release regardless? See [740 ILCS 14](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3004&ChapterID=57), [PA 103-0769](https://ilga.gov/legislation/publicacts/103/103-0769.htm) (V-excerpt); [Zellmer summary](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ninth-circuit-holds-face-signatures-used-for-photo-tag-suggestions-are-not-biometric-information) (V-secondary).
6. **MHMDA scope.** Are rep and hold metrics "measurements" of "bodily functions"? Does on-device-only processing avoid "collection"? Are the separate consumer-health-data policy and the dual consent needed? Do Nevada and Connecticut add anything? See [RCW 19.373](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373) (U).
7. **FTC HBNR.** If Delva combines user input with HealthKit, Health Connect or wearable data, is it a "vendor of personal health records"? What notification plan follows? See [FTC final rule](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/p205405healthbreachnotificationrule.pdf) (V-excerpt).
8. **AI Act role and scope.** Confirm minimal-risk status. Confirm that movement-based fatigue or effort features fall within recital 18's "physical states" exclusion. Confirm the effect of the Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744 as reported) on any future feature — [Recital 18](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/recital-18) (V-excerpt); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/) (V-secondary).
9. **Medical device line.** Would pain-driven program adaptation or "injury-safe" variants make Delva software a medical device under the EU MDR or UK rules? That would trigger Apple 1.4.1 proof, the Google medical declaration and AI Act Annex I. Not researched here; flagged by [Apple 1.4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (V-full) and [Google Play Health Content](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en) (V-excerpt).
10. **Model training.** Can body-only keypoints be anonymised well enough to fall outside the GDPR, or is consent (Art. 9(2)(a)) required for training on them? Does the Digital Omnibus GDPR package change this? The GDPR side of the omnibus was not verified (gap).
11. **UK specifics.** What did the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 change for automated decision-making and legitimate interests? Not researched (gap).
12. **Switzerland.** Confirm nFADP explicit-consent and DPIA duties for health data, and whether EDÖB guidance exists on fitness apps — [Fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en) (U).
13. **International transfers.** If derived data is hosted in the US, which transfer mechanism applies (EU-US Data Privacy Framework or SCCs)? Not researched.

### Inferences
- Questions 1, 3 and 5 decide the size of the consent UX and the DPIA. The others refine it.
