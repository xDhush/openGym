## H1. Credible open exercise databases and their licences (code vs data vs media)

### Takeaway
wger is the only mature, actively governed open exercise database. Its code is AGPL-3.0, its data carries Creative Commons licences per entry (CC-BY-SA 4.0 by default), and it stores attribution per object. The other popular datasets are smaller. They either have weak provenance (free-exercise-db, exercises.json) or bundle proprietary media under a permissive code licence (exercises-dataset, whose media belong to Gym visual; ExerciseDB, a paid API under its own terms). None models difficulty progressions or regressions, contraindications or coaching cues in a structured way. Delva will have to author those itself.

### Inferences
- Of the open sources, wger's CC-BY-SA data is the most defensible base if Delva wants to seed a catalogue. But share-alike means any **adapted** exercise text Delva publishes must also be CC-BY-SA, and each entry may carry a different author or licence. Given that Delva curates a small no-equipment set (5 programs), writing its own content will probably be simpler legally than adapting wger. That is an inference, not legal advice.
- "Public domain" claims (free-exercise-db, exercises.json) are only as strong as their undocumented provenance. Delva should not ship those images without its own provenance check.
- Every dataset reviewed lacks structured **regression/progression chains, contraindications, common faults and coaching cues**. These are exactly what Delva's form tracking and audio feedback (features 1 and 2) need. Delva has to author them.

### Gaps
- wger's exercise count, language coverage and image licence mix were not verified (wger.de and readthedocs blocked).
- The ExerciseDB terms of use (dub.sh link) were not read.
- Gym visual's own terms were not read; this matters for anyone licensing its media, as openGym and exercises-dataset do.

## H2. Ontologies, standards and platform exercise enumerations: what they model

### Takeaway
Formal standards model physical activity at a **coarse** level: activity type, minutes, days per week, METs. None of them codes individual exercises, cues or regressions. The exceptions are Android Health Connect's **exercise segments** (68 types, including PLANK, SQUAT, LUNGE and BURPEE, with a repetitions field), which map directly onto Delva's rep counting and hold timing, and EXMO, a new OBO Foundry ontology for exercise prescription. Apple HealthKit records only workout categories, plus lap/segment events.

### Inferences
- Delva's content model must go beyond every open schema reviewed. - Health Connect segments are the best platform target for exporting Delva's per-exercise reps and holds. HealthKit can store only the workout type plus segment or lap events.

- EXMO's paper venue and its class-level coverage of contraindications and progressions are unread.
- The current HealthKit enum and the Health Connect release that ships the newer fields are unverified.
- Compendium, PACO and OPE are unverified (see the cross-cutting Gaps section).

## H3. Content governance: validation, versioning, review and translation ("practices worth copying")

### Takeaway
wger has the most complete governance model for exercise content. Contributors must pass a trust gate (account age plus verified email). Every exercise, translation, image and video keeps an edit history. Exercises carry global UUIDs. A deletion log records which exercise replaced a deleted or merged one, so other installations can follow. Licence and attribution are stored per asset. UI strings are translated on Weblate, and exercise text is translated inside the app. Smaller projects add JSON-schema validation in CI (free-exercise-db) and draft/published/archived states with per-language fields (workout.cool). No open project reviewed has clinical or coach sign-off.

### Cited Findings

**Content governance practices worth copying (validation, versioning, review, translation)**

1. **Stable global IDs and variation groups.** Each exercise has `uuid` (unique, not editable, "to identify the base across installations"). Variants share a `variation_group` UUID ([wger base.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/wger/master/wger/exercises/models/base.py)) **[strong]**. *Delva:* programs should reference exercise UUIDs, never names, so content edits do not break program cells.
2. **Full edit history on every content type.** `HistoricalRecords` (django-simple-history) is attached to exercise base, translation, image, video, comment and alias models (GitHub code search `repo:wger-project/wger HistoricalRecords path:wger/exercises`, result files listed in [wger/exercises/models](https://github.com/wger-project/wger/tree/master/wger/exercises/models)) **[strong]**. *Delva:* keep versioned content, so a user's logged session points to the version of the cue or instruction that was live then.
3. **Deletion and merge log for downstream sync.** `DeletionLog` stores `model_type`, `uuid`, `replaced_by` ("UUID of the object replaced by the deleted one"), `timestamp` and `comment`, "to keep the local instances in sync with upstream wger" ([deletion_log.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/wger/master/wger/exercises/models/deletion_log.py)) **[strong]**. *Delva:* when an exercise is retired or merged, map old to new so in-progress chapters keep working.
4. **Per-asset licence and attribution (TASL).** The `AbstractLicenseModel` fields are `license` (default CC-BY-SA 4), `license_title`, `license_object_url`, `license_author`, `license_author_url` and `license_derivative_source_url`, and it builds a safe attribution link ([models.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/wger/master/wger/utils/models.py)) **[strong]**. Feeel stores `descLicense`, `descAuthors` and `imageLicense` per exercise ([exercise_tables.dart](https://gitlab.com/enjoyingfoss/feeel/-/raw/master/lib/db/exercise_tables.dart)) **[strong]**. *Delva:* even for commissioned media, record the creator, the licence or contract, the model release and the derivative source per asset.
5. **Trust gating for contributors.** `is_trustworthy`: superusers are trusted; temporary (demo) users never are; otherwise the account must be older than `MIN_ACCOUNT_AGE_TO_TRUST` days **and** have a verified email ([profile.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/wger/master/wger/core/models/profile.py)) **[strong]**. The docs say "your account must be at least 3 weeks old and have a verified email" ([contributing.rst](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/docs/master/docs/contributing.rst)) **[strong]**. Feeel says the 21-day rule exists "to cut back on spam" ([Feeel CONTRIBUTING](https://gitlab.com/enjoyingfoss/feeel/-/raw/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)) **[strong]**. *Delva:* its content is curated in-house, so the analogue is role-based editorial permissions (author, reviewer, approver), not community trust.
6. **Sync never overwrites local content.** "Sync commands never overwrite exercises or ingredients you've added yourself to your instance" ([sync-data.rst](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/docs/master/docs/administration/sync-data.rst)) **[strong]**.
7. **Schema validation in CI.** free-exercise-db lints every exercise JSON against `schema.json` (`make lint`), with nullable fields where data is incomplete, and runs Vitest/Cypress on the frontend ([repo](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db)) **[strong]**.
8. **Publication states and per-language fields.** workout.cool programs have `visibility` DRAFT/PUBLISHED/ARCHIVED (default DRAFT) and per-language title, description and slug fields (base, En, Es, Pt, Ru, ZhCn) ([schema.prisma](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Snouzy/workout-cool/main/prisma/schema.prisma)) **[strong]**.
9. **Translation split.** wger: "Translated by the community via Weblate" for the app ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/wger/master/README.md)); exercise translations are made in wger via "the language menu on an exercise's detail page" ([Feeel CONTRIBUTING](https://gitlab.com/enjoyingfoss/feeel/-/raw/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)) **[strong]**. Feeel: UI translated on Weblate ([README](https://gitlab.com/enjoyingfoss/feeel/-/raw/master/README.md)) **[strong]**. *Delva:* translate UI strings with a translation-management tool. Treat exercise cues and audio prompts as **content**, with per-language review, because spoken cues must be short, safe and unambiguous.
10. **Photo provenance rules.** Feeel: photos "must be your own work", source photos may be CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA, and contributions based on non-CC-BY-SA-compatible photos are refused. Licence info goes by email because "wger can't store license info for photos" ([Feeel CONTRIBUTING](https://gitlab.com/enjoyingfoss/feeel/-/raw/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)) **[strong]**. This is a cautionary example of provenance living outside the data system.

**Peer-reviewed work on the quality of exercise content in apps:** **not verified in this session** (journal hosts and search unavailable). Leads are in the cross-cutting Gaps section.

### Inferences
- What the open projects lack, and Delva needs, is **expert review as a recorded state**: content goes draft, then coach review, then (for safety-relevant items) physio or clinical review, then published, with the reviewer and date stored next to each version. None of the open projects records a qualified reviewer. That is a reasonable Delva differentiator and fits its "no perfect technique promise" rule.
- Delva's chapter rule ("approved shorter versions earn credit") needs *versioned variants* of a workout, approved by an editor and linked to the canonical version. A wger-style UUID plus history plus `replaced_by` model supports that.

### Gaps
- wger's moderation beyond trust gating (comment review, admin reverts in the UI, duplicate merging workflow) was not read; the docs site is blocked.
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of open exercise databases was found or verified.

## K1. Popular or credible open-source workout apps (other than openGym)

### Takeaway
The open-source apps closest to Delva's program model are workout.cool (program, then weeks, then sessions, with level, sessions per week and session minutes, advanced by sequence counters) and wger (routines that either "fit in week" on weekdays or run as a "custom cycle", plus a per-day "needs logs to advance" switch). The best progression designs are LiftLog's ordered rules and Liftosaur's DSL (programmable language). Feeel is the only real home/bodyweight app: timed intervals with text-to-speech, but no levels or programs. No open app implements Delva's dual chapter gate (work done **and** at least 14 active days), approved short versions, or check-in-driven frequency changes.

### Inferences
- **Sequence, not calendar.** workout.cool (numeric `currentWeek`/`currentSession`), wger's custom cycle and LiftLog's ordered sessions all advance by sequence, which supports Delva's "missed workouts never become debt" rule. Only wger offers an explicit "advance only if logged" gate, and its threshold (one set) is too lax for Delva.
- **No open app separates "work done" from "time elapsed".** Delva's chapter needs two counters (required sessions completed, and at least 14 active, non-paused days). That combination appears new among the open apps reviewed.
- **Completion integrity.** workout.cool's action does not enforce order or block re-completion. Delva should enforce idempotency (one credit per required workout), keep partial sessions as activity without credit, and record which approved short version earned credit.

### Gaps
- The workout.cool UI may enforce order; only the server action was read.
- Feeel's custom-workout editor and its F-Droid status were not verified.
- No open app was found that implements end-of-chapter check-ins or frequency-change suggestions.

## K3. How comparable apps structure plans, schedules, progression and missed sessions (Delva-relevant only)

### Takeaway
Open apps converge on **program, then weeks or cycle, then sessions, then exercises, then sets**. Each set target is reps or seconds, and the user advances by sequence. Progression is either declarative rules applied only after a fully successful session (LiftLog, wger requirements) or a scripting language (Liftosaur). Deloads, where they exist, are just weeks with progression switched off. Missed sessions are handled by not using calendars at all. No app has explicit partial-credit, approved-short-version or check-in rules.

### Cited Findings
- **Plan hierarchy with level, frequency and duration as program attributes:** workout.cool `Program.level`, `durationWeeks`, `sessionsPerWeek`, `sessionDurationMin`, then `ProgramWeek.weekNumber`, then `ProgramSession.sessionNumber`, then `ProgramSessionExercise.order`, then `ProgramSuggestedSet` (reps via `valuesInt` or seconds via `valuesSec`) — [schema.prisma](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Snouzy/workout-cool/main/prisma/schema.prisma) **[strong]**. Maps onto Delva's cell (program × level × 3/4/5 days); Delva's "chapter" would sit between program and week.
- **Calendar versus sequence as a user-visible choice:** wger "Fit in week" versus "Custom cycle" — [routines.rst](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/docs/master/docs/manual/routines.rst) **[strong]**.
- **Gate advancement on actual logging:** wger "needs logs to advance" (≥1 set logged), optional per day — [routines.rst](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/docs/master/docs/manual/routines.rst) **[strong]**.
- **Sequential counters without dates:** workout.cool advances `currentSession`, rolls to the next week, and finishes when completed sessions ≥ total — [complete-program-session.action.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Snouzy/workout-cool/main/src/features/programs/actions/complete-program-session.action.ts) **[strong]**.
- **Progress only after full success; one rule at a time:** LiftLog ("the first rule that still has room to move is applied - and only that one"; reps rules with a `ceiling` hand over to the next rule) — [PlanFileFormat.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LiamMorrow/LiftLog/main/docs/PlanFileFormat.md) **[strong]**. wger requirements ("the weight will stay the same until you do") — [routines.rst](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/docs/master/docs/manual/routines.rst) **[strong]**.
- **Bodyweight load modelling:** LiftLog `resistance: bodyweight | none | external` — [PlanFileFormat.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LiamMorrow/LiftLog/main/docs/PlanFileFormat.md) **[strong]**.
- **Deloads:** Liftosaur `progress: none` for a deload week — [liftoscript.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/astashov/liftosaur/master/docs/content/liftoscript.md) **[strong]**. Not documented in wger **[strong, absence in the doc read]**.
- **Failure handling:** Liftosaur `lp` can reduce weight after N failed attempts; custom scripts can switch set schemes on failure — [liftoscript.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/astashov/liftosaur/master/docs/content/liftoscript.md) **[strong]**. LiftLog `failureRest` — [PlanFileFormat.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LiamMorrow/LiftLog/main/docs/PlanFileFormat.md) **[strong]**.
- **Timed home workouts:** Feeel 30 s work / 10 s rest / 5 s countdown; per-exercise overrides (side plank 15 s per side) — [bundled_workouts.dart](https://gitlab.com/enjoyingfoss/feeel/-/raw/master/lib/db/bundled_workouts.dart) **[strong]**. Liftosaur set and rest timers with `auto` advance for EMOM/Tabata — [liftoscript.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/astashov/liftosaur/master/docs/content/liftoscript.md) **[strong]**.

### Inferences (what Delva should learn from or avoid)
- **Borrow:** the workout.cool hierarchy, with a Chapter entity added. Its completion predicate would be `requiredWorkCompleted && activeDays >= 14`, with paused days excluded. **Borrow:** LiftLog's "one rule after full success" for moving between bodyweight variants (e.g. add reps up to a ceiling, then move to the next harder variant), stated in plain text in the app. **Borrow:** Feeel's per-exercise time overrides and per-side entries for holds.
- **Avoid:** wger's "≥1 logged set advances the day". Delva's spec says partial workouts are saved as activity but do not count as completed required work, and only *approved* short versions earn credit. **Avoid:** workout.cool's lack of order and idempotency checks. **Avoid:** silent calendar-based "missed" states. All the reviewed open apps are sequence-based, which matches "no debt".
- **Missing everywhere:** end-of-chapter check-ins that suggest frequency changes, and an audit trail of user accept/decline. Delva needs its own design here (see Areas E and G).

### Gaps
- Exactly how Liftosaur's "next day" is chosen after a skipped workout, and LiftLog's rotation semantics, were not documented in the sources read.

## Licence red flags

### Takeaway
The main hazards are (1) proprietary media bundled in permissively licensed repos, (2) non-commercial pose models and body models hidden inside otherwise permissive pipelines, (3) undocumented provenance behind "public domain" claims, and (4) copyleft (AGPL/GPL) code and share-alike (CC-BY-SA) data and art.

### Cited Findings
- **Proprietary media under an MIT repo:** exercises-dataset's MIT covers only code, structure and text; the media are © Gym visual, and "Cloning this repository does not grant you any license to the media" — [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/LICENSE) **[strong]**. openGym's media come from the same vendor under a separate licence (brief context).
- **Paid or opaque data terms:** ExerciseDB data and media are behind separate "Terms of use" and "Pricing Plans"; the code is AGPL-3.0 — [repo](https://github.com/ExerciseDB/exercisedb-api) **[strong]**.
- **Non-commercial research only:** OpenPose — [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CMU-Perceptual-Computing-Lab/openpose/master/LICENSE) **[strong]**. It is used in OpenCap's cloud pipeline settings — [opencap-core](https://github.com/opencap-org/opencap-core) **[strong]**.
- **PolyForm Noncommercial plus SMPL non-commercial:** OpenCap Monocular — [repo](https://github.com/utahmobl/opencap-monocular) **[strong]**.
- **Custom or unclear evaluation licence:** Qualcomm FitCoach ("Software Evaluation License - Fitcoach.doc" versus BSD-3-Clause-Clear in the sidebar) — [repo](https://github.com/Qualcomm-AI-research/FitCoach) **[strong]**.
- **Copyleft code (AGPL-3.0):** wger, wger Flutter, Feeel, Liftosaur, LiftLog, ExerciseDB API. **GPL-3.0:** Ryot, openScale, GymMane (plus an additional attribution term) — see the tables **[strong]**. Delva should take ideas only, with no code, as it already plans.
- **Share-alike data and art (CC-BY-SA):** wger default data licence CC-BY-SA 4.0 — [constants.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wger-project/wger/master/wger/utils/constants.py). Workout Guide and Everkinetic art — [README](https://github.com/bryllim/workout-guide) **[strong]**. Commercial use is allowed, but adaptations must be released under the same licence, with attribution.
- **Undocumented provenance:** free-exercise-db derives from exercises.json, whose README states no sources and advertises a separate commercial dataset — [free-exercise-db](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db), [exercises.json](https://github.com/wrkout/exercises.json) **[strong]**.
- **Unstated content licence:** workout.cool code is MIT, but its exercise and video data source is undocumented — [README](https://github.com/Snouzy/workout-cool) **[strong]**.
- **Licence metadata mismatch:** the EXMO registry says CC BY 4.0, but the GitHub repo has no detected licence (`license: null` in the GitHub API) **[strong]**.

### Inferences
- The safest path for Delva is first-party exercise media (filmed or illustrated under work-for-hire contracts with model releases) plus first-party cue text, with any external data imported only from CC0 or CC-BY sources whose attribution is tracked.

### Gaps
- Licences for pose model weights and training datasets (e.g. RTMPose, MediaPipe BlazePose model cards) belong to the vision area and were not checked here.
